The Freelancer's 30-Minute Monthly Money Review

Most freelancers review their money only when something breaks: an overdue invoice turns 60 days old, or a quiet month collides with rent. By then the fix is damage control. A 30-minute monthly ritual catches every leak while it is still a puddle. Here is the exact sequence.

Minutes 1 to 5: scan the aging list

Open your invoice list (FreshBooks, Bonsai, Wave, HoneyBook, or your own spreadsheet) and sort every unpaid invoice by age, oldest first. You are looking for three buckets: under 30 days (normal), 30 to 60 (chase firmly), over 60 (escalate). Anything over 90 days gets a star; those invoices need a decision this session, not another reminder. If you do not have an aging view, make one: invoice number, client, amount, due date, days overdue, last contact. Five minutes, no analysis yet, just the facts on one screen.

Minutes 6 to 15: pick your top 3 overdue actions

Do not try to chase everything. Pick exactly three invoices: the one closest to a promised payment date, the largest one past 30 days, and the oldest one still unrecovered. For each, write the single next action: the exact email to send, the exact person to call, the exact date you escalate to a formal notice. Draft the emails now while you are in the mindset; scheduling three chases takes less time than thinking about ten. If an invoice is over 90 days and the client has gone silent, the action is not another friendly nudge, it is the demand letter or the small-claims filing decision.

Minutes 16 to 20: audit your terms on open proposals

Before any new work locks in, check the quotes and proposals still sitting unsigned. Does each one carry a due date, a stated late fee, a deposit line, and the correct billing contact? Freelancers lose more money in the easy first week than in any chase. One missing late-fee clause today is a 60-day headache in three months. Fix any proposal that is missing terms before you send the next follow-up.

Minutes 21 to 25: forecast the next 30 days

List every invoice you will send in the next month and every payment you expect to receive, with dates. Now look for the gaps: a week with no money arriving, a big invoice landing right when a client goes on holiday. Forecasting is not accounting, it is steering. One visible gap is enough reason to pull an invoice date forward, invoice a milestone early, or line up next month's work this week.

Minutes 26 to 30: close one leak permanently

Every month, pick one structural fix and do it now, inside the session. Candidates: turn on automatic payment reminders in your invoicing tool (FreshBooks, Bonsai, and Wave all have them on free tiers), add a payment link to your invoice template, update your contract's late-fee clause, or add a second payment method so a failed card never stalls an invoice. One leak closed per month is twelve fixes a year. That compounds into a payment system that mostly runs itself.

Make it stick

Put it on the calendar as a recurring event, same day every month, and treat it like a client meeting you cannot cancel. The first two sessions feel slow; by the third you will do it in twenty minutes, because the list gets shorter when every invoice has an owner and a next action.
