Stop Chasing Invoices and Fix the Payment Setup Instead

Most late payments are not a chase problem. They are a setup problem. The freelancer who gets paid on time usually did the work before the invoice existed: the right terms, the right rails, the right reminders. Here is the setup that quietly shortens how long clients take to pay.

1. Invoice from software that sends its own reminders

If your invoices go out as email attachments, every follow-up costs you willpower. Invoicing software with scheduled reminders removes that. Wave offers free invoicing with automatic payment reminders; most paid tools (FreshBooks, Bonsai, HoneyBook) do the same. Set three: a reminder three days before the due date, one on the due date, one seven days after. The software becomes the persistent one, and you stay the friendly one.

2. Give the client more than one way to pay

Every extra step between "I should pay this" and the money leaving their account is a place where payment dies. Bank transfer details alone are not enough for many clients. Add a card payment link (Stripe payment links work without a full checkout setup), keep PayPal as a fallback for international clients, and use Wise for cross-border payments where bank transfers are slow or expensive. State all options on the invoice itself, not in a separate email.

3. Put the due date where it cannot be missed

The due date goes in the invoice header, in bold, next to the total. Not in the terms paragraph at the bottom. Clients pay what is in front of them first. While you are there, add your late-fee terms in one short line ("Late payments accrue 1.5% monthly interest after the due date"), stated before the work, not after it is late.

4. Offer an early-payment incentive, not a late-payment threat

A 2% discount for payment within 7 days costs less than the hours you spend chasing, and it reframes the whole relationship: the client is gaining something by paying fast instead of dodging a penalty. Put it on the invoice: "2% discount if paid within 7 days." Some clients will take it every time. Those become your favorite clients, and the discount is cheaper than your chase time.

5. Send invoices the moment the work is done, and confirm receipt

The payment clock starts when the client sees the invoice, not when you send it. Invoicing the same day the work ships is the single highest-leverage habit in this list. Then confirm it landed: "Invoice attached for [project], due [date]. Please confirm you've received it." A confirmed receipt kills the "I never got it" stall line before it is born. If your software shows when the invoice was viewed, even better: no one can claim they missed it.

6. Keep terms identical everywhere

Contract, proposal, invoice, and your website should all say the same thing: same net terms, same late-fee line, same payment methods. Inconsistency is where disputes breed ("I thought it was net 30"). Write your standard terms once, save them as a template, and paste them unchanged every time.
