Charge for the Chase: How to Bill Your Collection Time Back to Late Clients

Every late payment costs you twice: once in the money that arrives late, and once in the hours you spend chasing it. The polite reminder, the follow-up call, the revised invoice, the email thread that runs to twenty messages. That time is real work. It is time you did not spend on billable projects. And in many cases, you can bill it back to the client who caused it.

Your chase time is billable time

Freelancers bill for research, revisions, and rush delivery without hesitation, then treat collections as a free hobby. Stop. Every hour spent chasing a late invoice is an hour of administrative labor caused by the client's breach of the payment terms. Plumbers charge for the callout. Lawyers charge for the demand letter. There is no reason a freelancer's chase time should be free.

The honest math: if your rate is $75 an hour and you spend three hours across a month chasing one overdue invoice, that is $225 of unbilled labor. A 1.5% monthly late fee on a $2,000 invoice is $30. The fee punishes the delay. The admin charge recovers the cost of the delay. You need both.

The admin-time line in your contract

You cannot surprise a client with a chase fee. The line has to be in the contract before the chasing starts. One sentence is enough:

Collection activity required by payments more than 14 days past due will be billed at $[X]/hour (or $[X] flat per incident), documented in writing, and added to the outstanding invoice.

The number should match your real rate or your stated admin rate. A flat per-incident fee ($50 to $150) is simpler and harder to argue with than an hourly log, especially with small clients. Pick one, write it in the contract, and enforce it exactly once to make it real.

What counts as chase time (and what does not)

Only count time that exists because the payment was late. A real log looks like this:

  • Drafting and sending payment reminders beyond the first one: 15 minutes each
  • Calls and voicemails about the overdue invoice: 30 minutes each
  • Reissuing, correcting, or re-explaining the invoice at the client's request: 30 minutes
  • Escalation emails, demand letters, preparing for collections or small claims: billed at full rate

What does not count: your first reminder, sending the original invoice, and time you spend being angry about the invoice. The rule is simple and defensible: the first reminder is free. Everything after that is labor.

The invoice format for collection charges

Add the charges to the original invoice or issue a separate "Invoice #24-A: Late Payment Administration." A separate line item is cleaner for the client's accounts team, which is often a different person from the one ignoring your emails. Format it exactly like any other line item:

Late payment administration: 4 chase reminders (15 min each), 2 calls (30 min each), 1 invoice reissue (30 min). Total: 2.5 hours at $75/hr = $187.50. Per clause 6 of our agreement.

Reference the contract clause. Attach the log. Keep the tone identical to a normal invoice: boring, factual, payable.

When NOT to charge

Charging for the chase is a tool, not a religion. Do not charge it when:

  • The late payment was genuinely your mistake (wrong amount, wrong recipient, sent to the wrong email).
  • The client communicated proactively about a real problem and gave you a firm pay date before the deadline passed.
  • The relationship is worth more than the fee. A great client with one late payment in two years gets grace. A chronic late payer gets the admin fee on invoice one of the new regime, and that is exactly how the regime becomes real.

What to do if they refuse

Some clients will refuse the admin charge while paying the invoice. Decide in advance what the charge is for: is it cost recovery, or is it a behavior signal? If it is cost recovery, accept the invoice payment and note the admin fee as waived in writing ("admin fee waived as a one-time courtesy"). The written note matters: it says the fee was real, you chose to forgive it, and the next one stands. If it is a behavior signal and they refuse twice, the client is telling you who they are. Raise their rate, shorten their terms, or walk away.

Start this month. Add the admin-time line to your contract template, log the chase on your next overdue invoice, and send the first admin invoice the moment a payment crosses 14 days late. The freelancers who get paid on time are the ones who make late payment expensive in every dimension, not just one.
