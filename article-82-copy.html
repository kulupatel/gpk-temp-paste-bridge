How to Answer "Can You Give Us 90 Days?": Negotiating Payment Terms Under Pressure

It usually comes at the worst moment: the contract is almost signed, the work is yours, and then the client's procurement email lands. "Our standard terms are Net 90. Let us know if that works." You are one sentence away from financing their cash flow for a quarter. Here is how to answer without losing the client or the deal.

Understand what they are really asking

Net 90 is rarely a preference. It is usually procurement policy: every vendor gets the same terms, and the person asking you did not write them. That matters, because it means you are negotiating with a system, not a person who dislikes you. Systems respond to structure, not to pleading.

The second thing to know: their "standard terms" are more flexible than they sound. Large companies grant exceptions constantly for vendors who ask well. The ask is routine to them. It only feels dramatic to you.

Never accept on the spot

The worst answer is an instant yes. It teaches procurement that your terms are whatever they propose. Buy yourself one sentence:

"Let me check what works with our billing cycle and come back to you today."

This is not a delay tactic, it is a negotiation reset. You now get to return with a structured counter-offer instead of a yes or a panicked no.

The counter-offer that works

Do not counter with "Net 30." That is a rejection of their whole system, and procurement will kick it upstairs where it dies. Counter with a structure that gives them most of what they want while protecting you:

"We can work with Net 60 on this one, with a 30% deposit due before kickoff and a pause clause if any invoice passes 60 days. That keeps us aligned with your cycle while protecting both sides."

Three elements do the work. The deposit moves real money before you start. Net 60 instead of 90 cuts your exposure by a third. The pause clause is your enforcement: it turns "terms" into something with teeth.

If they push back on the deposit, drop the rate question instead: shorter terms in exchange for the same price. Never trade both price and terms.

What to never concede

Three lines you do not cross, no matter how big the client:

  • No paid-when-paid. If their client pays late, that is their problem, not yours. You did the work for them.
  • No invoice aging that starts on approval. Terms should run from invoice receipt, not from whenever their AP team gets around to approving it. Approval delays are their inefficiency to fix.
  • No verbal terms. If the exception is real, it goes in the contract or the PO. Procurement people change; the contract does not.

The walk-away math

Sometimes the answer is no. Run the numbers honestly: a Net 90 client paying $5,000 is lending them $5,000 interest-free for three months. If you would not lend a stranger that amount, do not lend it to a client. The client who walks away over a deposit was going to be your worst payer anyway.

Say it plainly:

"We cannot do Net 90 on this engagement. We can do the deposit plus Net 60 structure, or we can revisit when your terms cycle allows. Happy to hold your slot for two weeks."

Professional, final, and it leaves the door open. Most of the time, procurement finds the exception.
