The Retainer Overrun: How to Bill Extra Hours Without a Payment Fight

A retainer is a container. When the month's work overflows it, the overrun invoice is where payment fights start: the client "thought the extra was included," the number surprises them, and suddenly a reliable payer turns slow. The fix is not better arguing. It is making the overrun visible before it exists.

Price the container, not just the hours

Every retainer agreement should state what happens past the boundary, in one line: "Hours beyond 20 per month bill at $X/hour, invoiced separately." Without that line, every overrun becomes a negotiation. With it, the overrun invoice is just arithmetic. Write the line into every new retainer; for existing ones, add it at the next renewal as a "clarification," not a confrontation.

Announce the overflow while it is happening

The worst overrun invoice is the surprise one. When you see the month crossing the line, send a three-line message mid-month: "Heads up: we are at 18 of 20 hours with two weeks left. The remaining requests will fall into the overrun rate of $X/hour. Want me to keep going, or should we pause some items?" This does two things: it gets written approval for the overage, and it forces the client to choose, which ends the "I thought it was included" argument before it starts.

Invoice the overrun separately and immediately

Never fold overrun hours into the next month's retainer invoice. A mixed invoice invites the client to dispute the whole thing, and the dispute delays the base retainer too. Send a separate overrun invoice the same week, with a one-line summary referencing the mid-month approval. Clean separation means a disputed overrun never holds your core income hostage.

The three overrun objections, answered in advance

"I thought this was included" dies against the written boundary line. "This is more than we expected" dies against the mid-month heads-up with their reply. "Can you just absorb it this time" is the dangerous one: absorbing it once re-prices your retainer permanently. If you must absorb, do it in writing as a one-time exception, never as a new norm.

Audit the retainer every quarter

If you hit overrun three months running, the container is the wrong size. Raise the base retainer instead of billing overruns forever; a higher predictable number beats a lower number plus monthly friction. Frame it as their win: "Your scope has grown, so I am sizing the retainer to match. Same work, no more surprise invoices."
