The Invoice With the Wrong Amount: How to Fix Overbills and Underbills Without a Fight

You hit send on the invoice, then looked at it again. The amount is wrong. Maybe you overbilled: the milestone was 2,000 and you typed 2,500. Maybe you underbilled: three days of work priced as two. Either way, your stomach dropped, because an invoice with the wrong number feels like it undermines your credibility.

It does not have to. How you handle the correction matters far more than the error itself. A fast, clean, honest correction builds trust. A discovered error, or a quiet hope that nobody notices, destroys it.

Fix it before they find it

Speed is the entire game. If you spot the error, correct it the same day, before the invoice enters their approval system. A self-reported correction reads as professionalism. An error the client's finance team finds reads as carelessness at best and dishonesty at worst. Never let a wrong invoice sit while you decide what to do; the decision is already made.

The overbill: correct it immediately

If you charged too much, send a corrected invoice right away and withdraw the old one. "Please disregard invoice #123; the corrected version is attached." One line, no drama. Do not let them pay the wrong amount with a plan to "sort it out later"; sorting it later means refunds, credit notes, and a finance contact who now double-checks everything you send. An instant correction costs you nothing and tells the client your numbers can be trusted.

The underbill: invoice the difference

This is the one freelancers get wrong. If you charged too little, you still get to invoice the difference. Send a short supplementary invoice for the balance with one honest line: "Correction to invoice #123: the total should have been X; this invoice covers the remaining Y." Most clients respect this; they know mistakes happen, and they would correct their own. Eating real money out of embarrassment is not professionalism. It is a subsidy you never agreed to.

The exact correction email

Keep it to three sentences. Subject: "Corrected invoice #123 (please use this version)". Body: "I spotted an error in the invoice I sent this morning: the total should be [correct amount], not [wrong amount]. The corrected invoice is attached; please disregard the earlier one and let me know if you need anything else." For an underbill, add: "The attached supplementary invoice covers the remaining [difference]." Short, factual, done. No long apology, no explanation of how the error happened.

What if they already paid

If they paid the overbilled amount, refund the difference immediately, same day, with a note identifying the invoice. Prompt refunds are memorable; slow ones are suspicious. If they paid the underbilled amount, send the balance invoice with the same honest line as above. Clients who have already paid are usually the easiest to deal with, because the relationship is already in good standing.

Prevent the next one

Errors come from rushing, not from incompetence. Build a two-minute invoice checklist: amount matches the quote or contract, quantities and rates are right, tax is applied correctly, the invoice number is sequential, the client's billing details are current. Use a template with formulas instead of mental math. For large invoices, read the numbers out loud once before sending; hearing "two thousand five hundred" when you meant two thousand catches what your eyes skip. The freelancers with the fewest invoice errors are not sharper; they just run the same check every time.
