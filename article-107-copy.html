The 5% Discount Offer: When "We'll Pay Now for Less" Is Worth Taking

Sooner or later a client offers you a deal: "What if we pay right now for a 5% discount?" It sounds reasonable, even tempting, especially when cash flow is tight. But not every discount offer deserves a yes. A disciplined freelancer answers it with math, not relief.

Do the actual math first

A 5% discount on a 30-day-early payment sounds small. Annualized, it is not. Giving up 5% to get paid 30 days early is the equivalent of paying roughly 60% annual interest on your own money. If your client would pay in two weeks anyway, the discount is pure loss. If they would have paid in 90 days, it is a different calculation. Before you answer, know the real terms: how late is this client usually, and how much is your time worth while you wait?

Discounts only buy speed, never loyalty

A client who asks for a discount on this invoice will ask for one on the next. That is the quiet cost most freelancers miss. So the rule: treat every discount as a one-time, invoice-specific arrangement. Say it explicitly: "I can do this as a one-time exception for this invoice." If you do not fence it, it becomes the new price.

When a yes makes sense

There are legitimate reasons to accept. Your cash flow is genuinely tight and the invoice is large. The client is reliably difficult, and a discount ends a cycle of chasing. You want to close the books on a relationship you are winding down. In each case the discount is buying something specific: certainty, speed, or closure. Know what you are buying before you agree.

Counter instead of accepting

You almost never have to take the first offer. "I can do 2% for payment today" keeps most of the money and still gives them the win of negotiating. Or trade instead of discounting: full price now, in exchange for a signed late-payment clause on the next project. The strongest freelancers rarely cut price; they trade price for better terms.

The discount that should always be no

Some offers are tests, not deals. "5% off if you skip the invoice" (no paper trail, tax problems), "10% off if you accept half the late fee as well" (they are negotiating your policy downward), or any discount demanded after you already started work. These are not about speed. Say no plainly, and treat the ask as information about how this client operates.
