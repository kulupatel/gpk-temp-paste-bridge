How to Track Every Invoice in 5 Minutes a Week

Invoices do not go unpaid because clients are evil. They go unpaid because nobody followed up on day 14. It happens to almost every freelancer: the invoice went out, life got busy, and three months later it turned up buried in a spreadsheet tab, unopened since it was sent. The fix is not discipline. It is a five-minute routine that runs whether you feel like it or not.

The 5-minute Friday routine

Pick one recurring slot, Friday morning works for most people, and run these four steps. Set a timer. If it takes longer than five minutes, your system is too complicated, not too simple.

Step 1: List every unpaid invoice. Open your tracking sheet (the layout is described below) and look at everything marked unpaid. Two minutes, no decisions yet.

Step 2: Flag what is overdue. Anything past its due date gets marked today. An overdue invoice with no flag is how money disappears quietly.

Step 3: Send the right chase for each one. One overdue invoice, one email, right now. If it is newly overdue, the gentle nudge. If you already sent the nudge, the firm follow-up. You will know which one, because the sheet tells you what you sent last time.

Step 4: Log the next action and the date. For every invoice you touched, write down what you sent and when the next follow-up goes out. "Nudge sent Sep 24, follow up Oct 1 if no payment." The routine fails the moment an invoice has no named next action, because "chase later" is how invoices die.

The six columns that matter

You do not need accounting software for this. A simple sheet with six columns covers it:

  1. Client name
  2. Invoice number
  3. Amount
  4. Due date
  5. Days overdue (a formula: today's date minus the due date)
  6. Next action ("Nudge sent Sep 24, escalate Oct 1")

Three things you can skip: hourly breakdowns (the invoice already has them), payment method notes (your bank knows), and color-coded priority systems (overdue or not is the only priority that matters).

The one rule that makes it work

Every overdue invoice gets a named next action, not a vague plan. "Chase client" is not a next action. "Send the firm follow-up email on Monday" is. The difference between freelancers who get paid and freelancers who write money off is not how polite their emails are. It is whether every invoice always has a next step with a date attached. When the list keeps growing despite the routine, the answer is escalation, not a better spreadsheet.

When the routine is not enough

The five minutes a week catches invoices at day 7 or 14, which is when most of them get resolved. But some invoices will sail past 30, 60, 90 days even with perfect tracking. That is a recovery problem, not a tracking problem, and tracking alone will never solve it.
