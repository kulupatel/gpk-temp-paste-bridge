The Payment Stack: 5 Tools That Chase Invoices for You While You Work

The best payment chase is the one you never have to think about. Not because your clients pay early out of gratitude, but because your system nags them on schedule without you lifting a finger. Here is how to build that system with tools that already exist, named specifically, with honest notes about what each one costs and what it actually does for you.

1. Stripe Invoicing: the free heavyweight

If you take card payments at all, Stripe's invoicing deserves a look. You can send an invoice, set automatic reminders, and let Stripe ping the client at day 7, 14, and 30 without you writing a single email. The reminders are clean, they carry the payment link, and they stop the moment the invoice is paid.

The catch: Stripe charges a processing fee per successful payment (around 2.9% plus a fixed fee in most regions), so every automated invoice costs you a cut. For freelancers billing hundreds or thousands per project, that is often cheaper than the hours you would spend chasing. The honest move is to build the fee into your rate once, then forget it.

2. FreshBooks: the invoice-chaser with memory

FreshBooks has been the freelancer favorite for a decade because it was built for exactly your life: sending invoices, watching which ones age, and nudging clients with automatic late-payment reminders. It tracks who has seen your invoice, which removes the "I never got it" excuse before it starts.

FreshBooks runs a paid subscription (it has limited free trials, not a permanent free tier), so it makes sense once you send at least a handful of invoices a month. The dashboard that shows every outstanding invoice in one place is the real value: a freelancer who can see the whole pileup at a glance chases faster.

3. Wave: the free one that covers the basics

Wave is free invoicing software with no subscription cost, which makes it the honest recommendation for freelancers just starting out. You can create invoices, accept payments, and see what is owed. It will not chase clients as aggressively as paid tools, but it gives you the paper trail and the payment links, which handle most of the problem at small scale.

The catch: Wave's advanced automation is limited, and payment processing still carries a per-transaction fee. Use Wave to build the habit of same-day invoicing; graduate to a paid tool when your volume justifies it.

4. Bonsai and HoneyBook: the all-in-one client systems

Bonsai and HoneyBook wrap contracts, invoicing, and payment reminders into one freelancer operating system. The client signs your contract inside the same tool that will later remind them to pay, which means your terms are never a separate PDF that went missing. Both send automatic reminders, and both let you require payment before delivery on milestone projects.

Both are paid subscriptions, so the honest math is simple: if you lose more than one invoice a year to disorganization, they pay for themselves. The clients who interact with a professional portal also tend to behave better, which is a real but unmeasured effect.

5. Your calendar: the free enforcement layer

Every tool above sends reminders. None of them tells you when to escalate, which is the part that actually ends late payment. Set a recurring 15-minute calendar block every Monday labeled "money review": open your outstanding invoices, and for anything past its terms, send the next escalation email manually. The tool handles the polite nudges; you handle the firm ones. That division of labor is the whole system.

The setup that works

Pick ONE tool from this list and commit to it for three months. Configure automatic reminders at 7, 14, and 30 days past the due date. Turn on read receipts or viewed-invoice tracking. Invoice the same day you deliver, every time. Then protect your Monday 15-minute block like a client meeting.
