The "Approved but Unpaid" Limbo: What to Do When Your Invoice Is Approved and the Money Still Is Not Coming

There is a special kind of frustration reserved for the invoice that everyone agrees is fine. Your contact confirms it. The work was delivered. The amount is correct. The invoice status says "approved." And then nothing happens for three weeks. No dispute, no complaint, no rejection. Just silence from the accounts team.

This is the approved-but-unpaid limbo, and it has its own rules. The tactics you use against a disputing client will not work here, because there is nothing to dispute. You are not fighting a person. You are fighting a process.

Why approval does not mean payment

Most freelancers hear "approved" and mentally file the invoice under "done." Inside the client's company, approval is only step one of three. First, your contact approves the work. Then, the invoice enters the accounts payable queue. Then, it waits for a payment run, which might happen weekly, fortnightly, or on a fixed monthly batch date. If your invoice was approved on the 2nd and the next payment run is the 28th, you will hear nothing for 26 days, and nobody considers anything wrong.

The second cause is the missing piece: a purchase order number, a cost-center code, a vendor onboarding form you never filled in. The invoice is approved in spirit but cannot be released by the system until the paperwork is complete. Nobody tells you this because, from inside the company, it does not look like a problem. It looks like a queue.

Step 1: Confirm exactly which stage you are stuck at

Send one short email to your contact, not to accounts. Keep it factual and ask for the two specific things you need:

Hi [Name], just checking on invoice #2026-114 for [amount], due [date]. It shows as approved on your side. Could you tell me (1) whether it has been passed to accounts payable, and (2) the next scheduled payment run date? Happy to fill in any missing forms if something is holding it up.

The payment run date is the single most useful fact in this situation. Once you know it, the waiting has an end date, and you can plan around it. If your contact does not know, ask them to introduce you to the specific person in accounts payable who handles your invoices. One named human in AP is worth ten reminder emails to a generic finance inbox.

Step 2: Fix the paperwork gap yourself

If the hold-up is a missing form, a PO number, or a vendor registration, treat it as your problem to solve today. Companies do not chase freelancers for paperwork. They let the invoice sit.

Ask accounts payable directly: "What exactly do you need from me to release this payment?" Then do it within 24 hours. Common blockers include a W-8BEN or W-9 tax form, a supplier onboarding form, a PO number your contact never raised, or an invoice submitted to the wrong email or portal. Each of these is a five-minute fix that unlocks a five-week delay.

Step 3: Get the payment run date in writing, then calendar it

Once you have the next payment run date, confirm it in a short email so it is written down:

Thanks, [Name]. So invoice #2026-114 should go out in the payment run on [date]. I will follow up on [date + 2 days] if it has not landed. Appreciate your help.

Two days after the run date, if the money has not arrived, follow up immediately. Do not wait another cycle. A missed payment run usually means something slipped, and the sooner you flag it, the sooner it gets added to the next run instead of the one after that.

Step 4: If this happens twice, change the terms

One limbo invoice is bad luck. Two is a system you are choosing to stay in. For repeat clients whose approval-to-payment gap is measured in weeks, negotiate at the next contract stage:

  • Ask for the payment run schedule before you start work, and set your invoice due date to land just before a run, not just after one.
  • Add a clause that payment is due within 14 days of approval, not 30 days of invoice, so the clock starts when their process finishes, not when you send the file.
  • Get a named AP contact at kickoff, the same way you would get a project contact. It is normal to ask. Large clients expect it.

The approved-but-unpaid limbo is not a conflict. Nobody is angry, and nobody is stalling you on purpose. That is exactly why it drags on: there is no urgency on their side. Your job is to supply the urgency with calm, specific questions, a paperwork fix done the same day, and a payment run date you hold them to.
