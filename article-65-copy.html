The "We Never Received Your Invoice" Excuse: How to Make Every Invoice Unlosable

It is the most common stall line in freelancing, and the most effective, because it is sometimes true. "We never received your invoice." It resets the clock to zero. The 30 days start over. You feel foolish for chasing, they feel justified in ignoring, and your payment slides another month.

The excuse works because most freelancers send invoices the way they send birthday cards: once, into the void, with no proof. Here is how to make every invoice unlosable, so the excuse dies the first time it is tried.

Why the excuse works

Accounts payable departments really do lose invoices. The email went to a manager who left. It landed in spam. It was attached to a thread nobody opens. When a client says they never received it, there is a decent chance they are telling the truth, which is exactly why the excuse is so useful to the ones who are lying. You cannot argue with it, because disproving a negative is impossible.

The fix is not to argue. It is to build a delivery system that makes the excuse impossible to use twice.

The four-proof delivery system

Send every invoice through four channels, and log each one:

  • Email with the PDF attached, to the billing contact (not your day-to-day contact, the actual person who pays). Subject line includes the invoice number and the amount.
  • A payment link in the email body, from your invoicing tool (Wave, FreshBooks, Stripe, or Square Invoices all generate these free). The client can pay without finding the attachment.
  • Upload to their vendor portal if they use one (Coupa, Ariba, Bill.com). Portal submission creates a timestamped record inside their system, which is the hardest proof to dispute.
  • A one-line confirmation request: "Please confirm you have this and that it is in the payment run." Most people reply. That reply is your receipt.

Log the date, the recipient, and the method in your invoice tracker. Fifteen seconds per invoice, and the excuse is dead forever.

Confirm the AP contact at kickoff

Most "lost invoice" situations start before the work does. You invoice the person you worked with, but that person does not process payments, forwards your email to AP three weeks later, and the clock never started.

At project kickoff, ask four questions and write down the answers: Who exactly receives invoices? What email address? Do you use a vendor portal or PO numbers? What is your standard payment cycle? This takes five minutes and prevents half of all lost-invoice cases. Put the answers in the project file, not your memory.

The 48-hour resend protocol

When a client claims they never received an invoice, do not restart the clock and do not apologize. Resend within 48 hours with proof attached:

"Resending invoice INV-1042 for $2,400, originally sent March 3 to ap@client.com and uploaded to your portal the same day. Payment was due April 2. Please confirm receipt and advise on the payment date."

Notice what this does. It states the original send date and the channels, attaches the proof, and restates the due date. It is polite, factual, and completely unarguable. The invoice was not lost; it is documented. The clock did not restart; it continued.

When it is actually true

Sometimes the invoice really did go to the wrong person. A manager forwarded it late, an inbox changed, a portal migration ate it. When your delivery log shows you sent it to the wrong address, own it instantly and fix it: resend to the right contact, confirm receipt the same day, and update your kickoff questions so it never happens again.

The difference between professionals and everyone else is not that their invoices never get lost. It is that they can tell, in ten seconds, exactly where each invoice went.

The bottom line

"We never received it" is only a reset button if you cannot prove delivery. Four channels, a logged trail, the right contact from day one, and a resend protocol that states facts instead of asking questions. Build this once and the excuse retires permanently.
