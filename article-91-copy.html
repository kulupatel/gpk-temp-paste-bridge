The Payment Terms One-Liner: The Exact Sentence That Belongs on Every Quote

Most freelancers send quotes that say everything about the work and nothing about the money. Deliverables, timelines, revisions, price. Then at the bottom, a cheery "Looking forward to working together." No payment terms at all.

That silence is expensive. When a client sees no terms on your quote, they hear your default terms. And their default is whatever their accounts department normally does, which is usually 30 to 60 days after they feel like sending the money. You never agreed to that, but you never disagreed with it either, so it wins.

Why the quote is the right place

The invoice is too late to negotiate terms. By the time you invoice, the work is done and your leverage is gone. The contract is the legal place, but many freelancers start work from a quote plus a handshake. The quote is the moment you have the most negotiating power: the client wants the work, they have not paid anything yet, and adding one sentence costs them nothing.

Put the terms where the money conversation starts, and the terms follow the project instead of chasing it.

The one-liner

Here is the sentence, adapted to your numbers:

"Payment terms: 50% deposit to begin, balance due within 14 days of invoice, with a 1.5% monthly late fee on overdue amounts."

That is the whole thing. Three clauses, one line, no legalese. It covers when money starts (deposit), when it finishes (14 days), and what happens when it doesn't (late fee). Most clients read it, shrug, and accept. The ones who push back are telling you something valuable before you do a single hour of work.

Adjust the numbers, keep the structure

The structure never changes, only the numbers:

  • Deposit: 30 to 50% for new clients, lower for long-term ones with a clean history.
  • Due window: Net 7 for small invoices, Net 14 for most work, Net 30 only for large corporate clients with real AP departments.
  • Late fee: a fixed monthly rate stated upfront. It almost never gets charged, because its job is prevention, not revenue.

Never leave any of the three out. A deposit with no due date gets you half the money and a slow chase for the rest. A due date with no deposit gets you paid on time, eventually. A fee with no stated terms gets you an argument.

Where it sits on the quote

Bottom of the quote, under the price, above your signature line. Same page, not a separate document, not fine print. It should be as visible as the price itself. Clients who accept your price are already saying yes to the money; the terms are just the delivery instructions.

If you use quoting software or a proposal template, make it part of the template so you never have to remember. The one-liner only works if it is on every quote without exception, because the one quote you skip is the one that goes late.

What to say when they push back

Three common objections, three answers:

  • "Can we do Net 30?" Answer: "I can do Net 30 on the deposit invoice and keep Net 14 on the balance." You conceded on paper and changed almost nothing.
  • "We don't pay deposits." Answer: "I understand. For new clients my standard is a deposit, but I can do milestone billing instead: we split the project into thirds and you pay each before the next begins." Deposits by another name.
  • "Can you remove the late fee?" Answer: "The late fee is just my standard line, it has never applied to a client who paid on terms. I am happy to keep it at a rate you are comfortable with, but I can't remove it entirely." Removing it signals that lateness has no price.

Notice what all three answers do: they trade the client's objection for a structurally equivalent term, never for nothing. Every concession is a swap.

The line that closes the loophole

If you add only one more sentence, make it this one: "Work pauses on accounts more than 30 days overdue."

Late fees motivate; pause clauses end the bleed. A client can absorb a fee and keep you waiting. They cannot absorb losing their designer, writer, or developer mid-project. This is the sentence that makes the one-liner enforceable, and clients respect it because it is obviously fair.
