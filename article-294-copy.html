1. The reply: the price is the work, not the exposure

The reply works because it refuses the premise, and the premise is the whole trick. The premise is that the price depends on who sees the work, and the price does not depend on who sees the work, and the price depends on what the work is worth to the client, and what the work is worth to the client is the result, and the result is the finished work, and the finished work is the freelancer's responsibility, and the responsibility does not shrink when the audience grows, and the audience does not do the work, and the audience does not check the work, and the audience does not pay the freelancer, and the audience is not a party to the invoice, and the invoice is between the freelancer and the client, and the client is the one who pays, and the client who pays in exposure has paid in nothing, and the nothing is the trap, and the trap is the rate, and the freelancer is the one who is underpaid.

So when the client says the exposure is worth a discount, the honest answer is that the exposure is worth whatever the client says it is worth, and the work is worth the rate, and the two numbers are not the same number, and the freelancer is not selling the audience, and the freelancer is selling the work, and the work has a price, and the price is the rate, and the rate is in the quote, and the quote was agreed, and the agreement is the answer, and the freelancer who has the agreement has the answer, before the message is ever sent, and the client who agreed to the rate in the quote cannot ask for an exposure price, because the agreement says otherwise, and the agreement is the record, and the record is the proof, and the proof is the answer, and the answer is the facts, and the date, and nothing else.
