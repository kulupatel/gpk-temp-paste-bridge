The Client Who Only Pays on Fixed Payment Runs: How to Time Your Invoices

Some clients never pay "when the invoice is due." They pay when the machine runs: every Friday, the 1st and 15th, the last working day of the month. Your Net 14 terms are a suggestion; the payment run is the reality. Fighting it wastes energy. Timing it gets you paid weeks earlier.

Find the run schedule in week one

Ask the exact question at kickoff, before you invoice anything: "When do you run payments?" Most finance people answer instantly, because it is routine for them. Write down the answer verbatim, with the day, the frequency, and the cutoff for submissions. "We pay every Friday, invoices in by Wednesday 5 PM" is all you need. This is the single highest-value question a freelancer can ask a new client.

Send invoices to arrive before the cutoff, not on the due date

If the run is Friday and the cutoff is Wednesday, an invoice sent Thursday sits for six days doing nothing. An invoice sent Monday rides the next run. The rule: invoice the moment the work is done or the milestone hits, and never batch invoices past a cutoff. Sending Tuesday "because the terms say Net 14" is negotiating against yourself; the money moves Friday either way, so get into the queue early.

Ask for the one exception

Most payment-run clients have an informal override: an urgent invoice the finance person pushes through manually. You will not get it for routine work, and you should not ask for it routinely. But knowing it exists matters for the one invoice that genuinely cannot wait, like a project-end invoice when you need cash flow. Ask once, use rarely.

The invoice that misses the run gets a same-day nudge

When an invoice misses a run through no fault of yours, say so the next business day: "I see this missed Friday's run, could it ride Wednesday's?" Short, polite, specific. Finance people deal in runs and dates; giving them the target run makes their job easier. Silence means your invoice waits a full extra cycle.

Put the run on your own calendar

Once you know the schedule, put every future run date on your calendar with a one-line reminder: "Acme run Friday, invoice in by Wed." A 30-second calendar habit replaces the anxiety of wondering when money arrives. You are not chasing the client anymore; you are boarding the train on time.
