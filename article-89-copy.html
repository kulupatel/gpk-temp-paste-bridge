The "Person Who Approved This Left" Problem: How to Chase an Invoice When Your Contact Is Gone

You send your regular payment reminder to your contact. It bounces. LinkedIn says they left the company three weeks ago. Your invoice was addressed to a person who no longer works there, and nobody else knows your invoice exists.

This is one of the most common reasons invoices die, and it is almost never a deliberate dodge. It is an ownership gap. Fix the gap and the money usually moves.

Your invoice died with their exit

When your only contact leaves, your invoice loses its internal champion. Nobody forwards it to the new person. Finance never saw it. The replacement is drowning in handover debt and an unknown vendor invoice is the last thing they will open. Every week you wait, the invoice sinks deeper into the nobody-owned pile.

The rule: the day you learn your contact is gone is the day you re-send the invoice to a real replacement. Not next week. That day.

The 5-minute recon: find the chain of ownership

Before you write anything, spend five minutes rebuilding the chain:

  • Search the company site and LinkedIn for the new person in your contact's old role, plus the AP or finance contact for vendor payments.
  • Check your contract or original SOW for the named billing contact, the PO number, and the approval authority. That document names the role, not just the person.
  • Email the general accounts or finance address with the invoice number, contract reference, and one line: "My contact [Name] appears to have left; could you route this to whoever owns vendor payments now?"

CC the new person if you find them. One email, three destinations, paper trail restarted.

The handover email that gets you paid

When you reach the new contact, do not lead with frustration. Lead with a clean package that makes it easy to say yes:

Subject: Outstanding invoice #1042 ($3,400) from [Your Name], approved by [Old Contact]

>

Hi [New Contact],

>

[Old Contact] approved this work before their departure. I am re-sending the full package so nothing is lost in the transition:

>

- Invoice #1042 ($3,400), dated [date], terms Net 15
- Signed statement of work
- The delivered work attached for reference

>

Could you confirm receipt and let me know where this sits in your payment cycle? If the PO needs re-issuing in your name, tell me and I will re-paper it today.

You are doing their job for them: package, context, and an exact next step. New hires say yes to people who reduce their load.

The three-contacts rule (prevention)

From now on, every engagement gets three contacts, not one:

  • The day-to-day contact (your champion)
  • The person who signs or approves the spend (the authority)
  • The AP or finance person who actually releases payments (the wallet)

You collect all three at kickoff. Ask: "Who approves this invoice, and who should I send it to in AP?" It takes thirty seconds and it is the single highest-ROI sentence in this whole playbook. When one person leaves, you still own two live paths to the money.

What not to do

Do not keep emailing the departed contact hoping they forward it. People who leave do not monitor their old inbox for your sake, and forwarding your invoice is not their job anymore. Do not wait for the replacement to find you. And do not let the invoice age past 60 days while you "wait for things to settle": at 60 days it moves from the new contact's desk to the hard-problem pile.
