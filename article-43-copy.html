The 2-Email Sequence That Revives a Dead Invoice

A dead invoice is not the one that is five days late. It is the one that is 30 or more days past due, you have already nudged twice, and the client has gone quiet. Not angry, not negotiating, just silent. Most freelancers handle this one of two ways: they keep sending the same polite reminder every two weeks, or they give up and mentally write the money off.

Both are mistakes. The polite loop teaches the client that silence works. Giving up hands them a free discount on your labor. A dead invoice needs something different: two short emails, spaced apart, with a real escalation between them. Then you stop emailing.

What makes an invoice "dead"

An invoice becomes dead when the normal chase stops producing any response at all. You have sent the due-date reminder and at least one follow-up. The client has not answered, promised a date, or disputed anything. They are just gone.

This matters because the strategy changes. Early chasing is about making payment easy and visible. Dead-invoice chasing is about forcing a decision: the client pays, names a date, or reveals they are never paying. All three are useful. Silence is the only useless outcome.

Email 1: The Warm Restart

Send this 5 to 7 business days after your last contact, when the invoice is at least 30 days overdue. Its job is to give the client a graceful way back. Most late payments at this stage are not malice. They are invoices buried in an inbox, a bookkeeper on leave, or a client who felt awkward after missing the deadline. The warm restart removes the awkwardness.

Subject: Quick check: Invoice #1042 (March web project)
Hi [Name],
I am checking in on invoice #1042 for $2,400, sent March 5, which was due April 4. I know things get buried, so I wanted to resend it directly.
Can you let me know a payment date by Friday? If you need the invoice in a different format, or if there is anything on the line items to discuss, tell me and I will sort it.
Thanks,
[Your name]

Four rules make this work. Name the exact invoice, amount, and dates, so there is nothing to look up. Ask for one thing only: a payment date by a specific day. Offer face-saving exits (wrong format, disputed line items) so silence cannot be blamed on confusion. Stay warm: no scolding, no "as per my previous email." An awkward client gets permission to re-engage. A stalling one just lost their best excuse.

The gap: wait 5 to 7 business days in silence

After the warm restart, do nothing for a full 5 to 7 business days. No follow-ups, no LinkedIn likes, no "just bumping this." The gap is the mechanism: it signals the last message was not routine and that something different is coming. Log the send date and the deadline for your own follow-through. Send email 2 late and the sequence loses its teeth.

Email 2: The Final Notice

If the deadline passes with no reply, send the final notice. This email does three things the warm one did not: it names a concrete next step, attaches a real date, and tells the client this is the last email about the matter.

Subject: Final notice: Invoice #1042, $2,400 (action needed by May 20)
Hi [Name],
I have not received a response on invoice #1042 for $2,400, due April 4. This is my last email about it.
Unless payment is received by May 20, I will be moving this to the next step: a formal demand letter, and late fees of 1.5% per month as stated in our agreement, applied from the original due date.
If payment is already on the way, please send confirmation today so I can close this out.
[Your name]

The critical rule: only promise what you will actually do. If you do not intend to send a demand letter or apply late fees, do not mention them. A bluffed final notice that produces nothing teaches the client your deadlines are decorative. You get exactly one chance to make a final notice believable. Spend it on a consequence you will carry out.

When to stop

This is the part most freelancers get wrong. After the final notice, stop emailing. If 10 to 14 days pass with no payment and no substantive response, you have exactly two moves left: one formal demand letter with a deadline, or a conscious write-off with the lesson recorded. Stopping is not surrender; it is the recognition that email has done everything it can.

The bottom line

A dead invoice gets two emails: a warm restart that removes every excuse, and a final notice with one consequence you will carry out. Then you stop and escalate, or write it off on purpose. The sequence works because it is short, each email asks for one thing, and the silence between them says more than a third nudge ever could.
