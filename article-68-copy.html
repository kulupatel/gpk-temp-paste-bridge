The Deposit Follow-Up: What to Do When a Client Goes Quiet After Saying Yes

The client said yes. You sent the deposit invoice. Then silence. No payment, no questions, no cancellation. Just the quiet void where your confirmed project used to be.

This is one of the most common payment failures in freelancing, and it has nothing to do with the quality of your proposal. It is about momentum. A "yes" is emotional. Paying a deposit is operational. Between the two sits a gap where doubts, distractions, and competing priorities live. Your job is to build a small bridge across that gap, then let the client walk it.

Why the yes-to-deposit gap exists

Saying yes costs nothing. Sending money requires the client to find your invoice, open their banking app, maybe get an approval, and commit. Every one of those steps is a place to get distracted. Add normal business chaos and your project slides from "exciting new thing" to "that invoice I should probably pay this week."

It is almost never personal. The client is not testing you, renegotiating, or secretly ghosting. They are busy, and your invoice is sitting in a pile of other demands. Treat the silence as logistics, not a verdict, and your follow-up stays calm and effective.

The 48-hour check-in

If the deposit has not arrived within two business days of the invoice, send one short message. Keep it warm and assume good intent:

"Hi [Name], just floating the deposit invoice back to the top of your inbox so we can lock in your start date. Let me know if you have any questions about it."

That is the entire message. It does two jobs: it reminds them the invoice exists, and it ties payment to something they want (their start date). Most silent-yes situations resolve at this step, because the only real problem was inbox burial.

The one-week follow-up with a consequence

If a week passes with no payment and no reply, the tone shifts. You are not angry. You are informing them of a fact:

"Hi [Name], I wanted to let you know I am holding your project slot until [date]. If the deposit has not come through by then, I will need to release the slot and we can rebook when you are ready. Happy to answer any questions in the meantime."

Notice what this does. It does not threaten. It states a policy. Slots are finite, and holding one for a non-paying client costs you real opportunities. The date creates urgency without drama, and "happy to answer any questions" leaves the door open in case something is genuinely stuck.

When the client goes quiet for real

Sometimes even the deadline message gets no answer. At that point, close the loop professionally:

"Hi [Name], I have released your slot as planned. If you would like to restart the project, just let me know and we can set a new timeline. Thanks for considering me."

Then stop. No more chasing, no resentment, no "just checking in" messages every month. A client who cannot manage a deposit payment will be a client who cannot manage milestone payments either. You have filtered a problem before it became one.

The prevention system

The best deposit follow-up is the one you rarely need. Three habits shrink the gap to almost nothing:

First, send the deposit invoice during the call, not after it. The moment the client says yes, say "Great, I will send the deposit invoice while we are still on the line." Momentum is highest right now. Use it.

Second, make the payment frictionless. The invoice should carry a direct payment link with at least two methods (card and bank transfer, or card and PayPal). Every extra step the client has to figure out is another reason to do it later.

Third, state the deposit rule in writing before the yes. Your proposal should already say: work begins when the deposit clears, and the slot is held for seven days. When the policy was agreed up front, the follow-up is just an enforcement of a promise, not an awkward new demand.

The takeaway

A quiet client after a yes is not a crisis. It is a normal part of selling. The follow-up sequence, warm at 48 hours, firm at one week, closed after the deadline, handles it with professionalism and zero anxiety. And the prevention system, invoice during the call, frictionless payment, the rule in writing, means most of your yeses will convert without you ever needing the sequence at all.
